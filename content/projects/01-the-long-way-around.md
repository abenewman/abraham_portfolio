## Overview

In a kingdom built on strength and honor, stubborn, young blacksmith Hazel Briar believes her worth comes from one simple truth: if something is broken, you fix it. When quiet and thoughtful Prince Rowan arrives with a broken legendary sword– once said to guarantee victory– Hazel sees her chance to prove herself; but there’s one problem: she can’t seem to fix the sword. 

Desperate and spurred on by impatient Lord Garrick, a ruthless general bent on war, Hazel and Rowan journey to find the one person who might know more, only to find they were wrong about the origins of the blade’s shattering the whole time. Faced with new ideas about forging, they race home to try something bold, but with the threat of war rising and Lord Garrick demanding she forge a new weapon, Hazel faces an impossible choice: prove her strength the only way she knows how, or redefine what strength means entirely. 

In the end, Rowan stops running from his responsibilities, forced to make a stand, and Hazel doesn’t create a weapon– she crafts something new, forcing a kingdom built on glory to choose between power and peace.

> "You can't fix everything because some things were meant to be broken."

## Key Characters

Hazel: a strong-willed blacksmith who believes her purpose comes from her ability to build and fix things.

Rowan: a prince who feels like an outsider in his war-centric kingdom and unfit for the responsiblites that come with his title.

Lord Garrick: the lead general of the kingdoms armies and single-minded in his pursuit of glory through strength and victory.

## Key Locations

The Castle: Polished and organized, the casle is the epitomy of strength. Perfectly drilled soldiers, an authorative king, and a citadel that speaks for generations of vicotry in war, the castle reinforces ideals of glory and purpose. 

Holt: Simple, woodsy, and thriving, Holt is a village built on its forge district. Its narrow cobblestone streets culminate in a cluster of forges with blacksmiths hammering day and night. Holt's ordered chaos forces Hazel and Rowan into action. 

The Cabin: Whimsical, ancient, and surrounded by nature, the grandfather's cabin is found on the edge of the kingdom and challenges the rigid way of life imposed by the castle, presenting new ideas to Hazel and Rowan about how they choose to live their lives. 

> "Strength isn't about force; it's about knowing when not to use it."

## Development / Behind the Story

This story came about from an initial idea I had about a young blacksmith who put their value in their ability to create things. Hazel Briar quickly took form as a stubborn blacksmith, and Prince Rowan, a soft-spoken Prince, naturally took form soon after to complement her weaknesses. Their dynamic and mutual growth built the world for me, and as their characters got more defined, the story wrote itself through their actions and reactions to basic conflicts. I immediately loved how neither character was saved by the other, but that they mutually helped each other grow, and how the romance springs from mutual respect rather than people who don’t get along forced to work together.
