## Project Overview

Nine inhabited star systems are connected by the Skyfall Chains: a vast network of stations and energy links that make travel, trade, and daily life possible. Each system has developed its own culture and government around the resources it controls and distributes to the rest of the star systems. These Skyfall Chains are promoted as regulatory systems for the rapidly deteriorating stars at the center of each system, but in actuality they were created to centralize power and wealth. 

In the industrial Ash Ring, Rai Ash enters the Ascension Trials hoping to gain the power to protect the people he loves. As he encounters candidates from the other rings, he begins to confront a question at the heart of their civilization: what happens when the systems everyone depends on serve interests they cannot see? The Ascension Trials are meant to weed out the weak and promote those worthy of ruling, but Rai soon finds himself questioning the authenticity of the Chains as he uncovers a hidden ancient history and finds himself at the center of a civil war, and perhaps a more dangerous threat lurking just around the corner.

## My Role

I am developing The Skyfall Chains with my co-creator, Caleb Kira. My contributions include The Skyfall Chains, the core plot structure, the Ascension Trials, and the history of the world. We collaborate on character creation, brainstorm the story together, and maintain and craft a shared world guide detailing the different governments, the world's technology, and more. 

## The World

The Skyfall Chains connect each star system’s stations, planets, and trade routes. They also regulate the flow of energy from the central stars, making them essential to the societies built around them. Access to a chain can determine where people live, the flow of energy, how goods move, and which rings hold power.

Each ring responds to that shared infrastructure differently. Ash is an industrial system shaped by class divisions; Rowan’s mining companies compete for control of raw materials; Willow prizes education and the preservation of knowledge; and Blackthorn’s black markets offer access to technologies banned elsewhere. Those differences create opportunities for cooperation, conflict, and stories that extend beyond a single planet.

> "The removal of hope creates desperation, and desperation breeds success."

## Selected Work

World guide excerpt: Blackthorn Ring

Blackthorn is the place people go when they need something the other rings prohibit. A fallen oligarchy, the Blackthorn Ring is ruled by money. Its wealthy elites maintain private forces, putting them above the almost non-existent law, while smugglers and scavengers make use of goods the rest of the systems discard. The system is a living embodiment of the State of Nature, and as such, is a hub for intergalactic bounty hunters, drug smuggling, illegal AI use, forgery, thieves, and outlaws. Blackthorn also serves as the universal dump. Systems can pay a small fee and dump all their discarded trash on one of Blackthorn’s planets, and then it is combed through, and anything of use is scavenged. 

Blackthorn Ring is in such a fallen, lawless state because it was nuked as a ring early on in the system's development. Sentient AI was first created and implemented on Blackthorn Ring, and as it was rapidly implemented everywhere, fear of its power grew throughout the other systems. Through a fear-mongering campaign, several rings came together and cleared Blackthorn Ring using nuclear weapons, in an attempt to wipe out AI sentience. However, AI can still be found in the underground black markets that remain pervasive throughout the Ring.

## What it Taught Me

Crafting a world across nine connected star systems has challenged the scope of my world-building capabilities and pushed me to ask how each choice affects the characters who live within it. Working with another writer improved my clarity when explaining ideas, responding to and giving feedback, and keeping a shared world guide and story consistent and organized as we continue to develop it.
